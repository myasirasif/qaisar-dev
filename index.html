How a project runs

  1. Brief and scope

    We agree the page list, who supplies the content, and who will be editing the site once it's live. That last answer decides how the whole thing gets built.

  2. Build

    Theme or builder setup, then pages built out. Responsive from the first screen rather than patched on at the end, and checked across browsers as I go.

  3. Review on staging

    You get a staging link and send everything back in one pass. Feedback in a single list beats twenty messages, and it keeps the timeline honest.

  4. Launch and handover

    Deploy through cPanel, set redirects, confirm analytics fires, then walk you through editing the parts you'll actually want to change.

Good to know

Can I edit the site myself afterwards?
That's the point. Content sits in fields and blocks you can reach from the WordPress admin, and I walk you through the parts you'll touch most before handover.
Do you take over sites someone else built?
Yes, and it's a good part of the work. Usually it starts with broken mobile layouts, a plugin conflict, or redirects nobody set after a rebuild.
Which builder do you prefer?
Elementor for most client sites, Bricks when performance matters more, and plain HTML and CSS when a builder would only add weight to a simple page.
Who handles hosting and deployment?
I work directly in cPanel — files, staging, SSL, redirects — so you don't need a separate person for the launch.
How long does a site take?
A small business site is usually two to three weeks once content is ready. Stores and multilingual builds take longer, and I'll say so before we start rather than halfway in.
What timezone are you in?
PKT (UTC+5), and I've worked with NZ, Australian and European clients throughout, so overlapping hours are already part of the routine.
